## Summary

Computer-vision architectures evolved from hand-engineered features to convolutional networks, region-based detectors, segmentation networks, vision transformers, and multimodal models.

## Early era

Classical systems relied on edges, corners, filters, histograms, and geometric matching. They could work well in constrained settings but required substantial feature engineering.

## Deep learning shift

Convolutional neural networks learned hierarchical visual features directly from data. This enabled large gains in image classification, detection, segmentation, and representation learning when paired with larger datasets and GPUs.

## Modern direction

Vision transformers, self-supervised learning, segmentation foundation models, and vision-language models expanded the scope from single-task image models to reusable visual representations and multimodal reasoning.

## Why the sequence matters

Each architectural generation changed the engineering bottleneck: from feature design, to labelled data and compute, to scale, transfer, and evaluation across domains. That history helps explain why modern vision systems often combine pretrained backbones with task-specific heads.

## Historical lesson

Architectural progress repeatedly came from matching inductive bias, data scale, and compute. The best model family depends on task, labels, latency, and deployment domain.
