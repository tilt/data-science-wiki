# From N-gram Language Models to Transformers

## Summary

Language modelling evolved from counting short token histories to neural sequence models and then transformers that learn contextual representations at scale.

## Timeline

- N-gram models estimated next-token probabilities from fixed local histories and sparse count tables.
- Neural language models replaced sparse counts with learned dense representations.
- Recurrent models carried state through a sequence but were hard to parallelize over long contexts.
- Transformers used attention to condition on many prior positions in parallel, enabling large-scale pretraining.

## Historical mechanism

N-gram models estimate next-token probabilities from fixed local histories. Neural language models replaced sparse count tables with learned representations, and transformers added scalable attention over long contexts.
