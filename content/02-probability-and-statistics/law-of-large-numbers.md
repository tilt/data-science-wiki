## Summary

The law of large numbers says that sample averages converge toward expected values as the number of independent observations grows. It explains why larger samples usually produce more stable estimates.

## Core idea

If observations are independent and drawn from the same distribution with finite expectation, then the sample mean approaches the population mean:

$$
\bar{X}_n \to E[X]
$$

as $n$ increases.

## Example

A fair coin may show 7 heads in 10 flips, but over 100,000 flips the fraction of heads will usually be close to 0.5. The short-run sequence can be noisy even when the long-run average is stable.

## Practical use

The law supports empirical risk minimization, survey estimation, metric tracking, and simulation. More data reduces random sampling noise, assuming the data is representative and generated by the same process.

## Failure modes

More data does not fix biased sampling, label leakage, concept drift, dependence, or measurement error. A huge biased dataset can converge confidently to the wrong target.
